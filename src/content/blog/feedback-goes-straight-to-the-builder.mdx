Most apps put a wall between you and the people who make them. You file a ticket, a system replies, and your idea lands in a backlog you never hear about again. InnerZero works differently, because InnerZero is built by one person, and your feedback comes directly to me.

This post explains where the feedback box lives, who reads what you send, and what actually happens to a good idea after you press send.

## How do I send feedback or a feature idea in InnerZero?

Open the app and look at the sidebar. Under the "Join our community" block there is a "Give feedback or suggestions" option. Click it, type your message, and send. That is the whole flow.

The box is deliberately plain. You get a text field with a 2000 character limit and a live counter, a send button, and a short thank you when the message goes through. There is no form to fill in, no category dropdown, and no request for your name or email. You write what is on your mind and it reaches me.

If you are offline, or you have InnerZero in Offline privacy mode, the box tells you so instead of pretending to send. Nothing is lost or faked. You send it later when you are back online.

## Who actually reads my feedback?

I do. I build InnerZero on my own, so when you send a message it comes to me and no one else.

That is the part I want to be honest about, because it changes what your feedback is worth. There is no first-line support team filtering messages before a developer sees them. There is no product manager deciding which reports are worth passing on. The person reading your bug report is the same person who will open the code and fix it. The person reading your feature idea is the same person who decides what gets built next.

I wrote more about this setup in [who builds InnerZero](/blog/who-builds-innerzero) and [why I built InnerZero](/blog/why-we-built-innerzero) in the first place. The short version is that a small operation has one real advantage: the distance between "a user noticed something" and "it is fixed" is very short.

## Will you actually build what I suggest?

I read every suggestion, and a lot of what ships started as a message from someone using the app. I cannot promise to build everything, and I would rather be straight with you than pretend otherwise.

Here is how I think about it. When several people ask for the same thing, that is a strong signal and it moves up the list fast. When one person describes a rough edge I had not noticed, that is often the most valuable message of all, because it is easy to go blind to your own app. When an idea is a good fit for what InnerZero is, a private assistant that runs on your machine, it has a real chance. When an idea would push InnerZero toward being something it is not, I will usually say no, and I try to explain why.

Bug reports get priority over everything. If something is broken, I want to know, and a clear report with the steps that triggered it is the fastest route to a fix. Many of the fixes in recent releases came from exactly these messages.

## What should I send, and where?

Send anything that would make the app better for you. It helps to know which channel fits which kind of message, so here is a simple guide.

| You want to | Best channel | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Suggest a feature or improvement | Feedback box in the app | Comes straight to me, tied to nothing but your words |
| Report a bug you can reproduce | Feedback box in the app | Include the steps and I can usually fix it faster |
| Ask a question or chat with other users | Discord community | Other people using InnerZero can help too |
| Share a quick reaction or idea in passing | Either | Whatever is in front of you when the thought lands |

You do not have to write a formal report. "The voice panel felt cramped on my laptop" is a genuinely useful message. So is "I wish I could do X". Rough notes are fine, because I would rather hear the raw thought than nothing at all.

If you are still deciding whether InnerZero fits how you work, [the things you can do with InnerZero](/blog/things-you-can-do-with-innerzero) is a good place to start, and you can always send feedback once you have tried it.

## Does my feedback stay private?

Yes. The feedback box sends the text you type and nothing else. It does not attach your name, your email, your chat history, or your files. It does not track you, and the app keeps its own logs free of the message content.

This matches how the rest of InnerZero treats your data. The app runs on your machine, your conversations and memory stay local, and a feature that reaches out to the network, like this one, only sends the small, specific thing it needs to. In this case that is the message you chose to write. If you would rather talk in the open instead, the Discord community is there for that.

I mention this because privacy is the whole point of InnerZero, and a feedback box should not be the one place that quietly breaks the promise. It does not.

## What happens after I press send?

Your message arrives with me, and it becomes part of how I decide what to work on. Some messages turn into fixes in the next release. Some become features weeks or months later. Some I reply to in my head with "fair point" and carry around until the right moment to act on them.

I cannot reply to every message one by one, because the box is one-way by design and does not ask for your address. What I can promise is that it is read by a real person who cares about getting the app right, and that your idea is genuinely on the table. The best way to see the result is to keep an eye on the [changelog](/changelog), where the features and fixes that came from feedback show up release after release.

If you have been holding on to a suggestion, or you spotted something that felt off, open the app and send it. It reaches me, and it matters.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the feedback box in InnerZero?

It is in the app sidebar, under the "Join our community" block, labelled "Give feedback or suggestions". Click it, type your message, and send.

### Does sending feedback require an account or email?

No. The feedback box asks for nothing but your message. There is no sign in, no account, and no email field. It sends the text you write and nothing else.

### Who reads feedback sent through InnerZero?

I do. InnerZero is built by one person, so your message comes straight to me rather than through a support team or a ticket system.

### Can I report a bug through the feedback box?

Yes, and bug reports get priority. Describe what happened and the steps that led to it. A clear report is often all I need to reproduce the problem and fix it in a later release.

### Will my feature request definitely get built?

I cannot promise that, but I read every request and many shipped features started as one. Requests that several people ask for, or that fit what InnerZero is, have the best chance.

### Is my feedback private?

Yes. The box sends only the text you type, with no name, email, history, or files attached, and the app keeps its logs free of the message content. It works the same way as the rest of InnerZero, which keeps your data on your machine.
