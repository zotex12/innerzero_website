Health questions are some of the most private things people ask an AI, and some of the least private to send to a cloud service. Symptoms, medications, mental health, a worry you have not told anyone: most people would think twice before typing those into a chatbot that uploads everything to a server. InnerZero runs the AI on your own machine, so you can ask without that upload. It is worth being just as clear about what it is not, which is a doctor.

> **Quick summary**
> - Health questions are deeply personal, and cloud assistants send everything you type to a server.
> - InnerZero runs locally, so your questions about symptoms or medication stay on your machine.
> - It can help you understand terms, prepare for appointments, and think things through.
> - It is not a doctor and not a diagnosis. For anything serious, see a real professional.

## Is it safe to ask AI about health questions?

It depends entirely on where the AI runs. The information itself is sensitive, so the real question is who else sees it. With a cloud assistant, your symptoms and worries travel to a company server to be processed, where they can be stored or logged. With InnerZero, the model runs on your own hardware, so the question is answered on your machine and is not uploaded to do it.

That makes the privacy side genuinely safer. It does not make the AI a medical authority, which is a separate point covered below. Safe to ask privately, yes. Safe to treat as a diagnosis, no.

## Why does privacy matter so much for health questions?

Because health data is about as personal as it gets, and you cannot un-share it. A note about a mental health struggle, a question about a medication, or a worry about a symptom is the kind of thing people keep private for good reasons, from insurance to employment to simply not wanting it on a server somewhere. Once it is uploaded, you are trusting that service to hold it, and you cannot take it back. The general case for keeping sensitive material off the cloud is in [offline AI for sensitive work](/blog/offline-ai-for-sensitive-work).

Running locally removes the dilemma. You can ask the awkward, personal, half-formed health question you would never type into a cloud chatbot, because it never leaves your computer. The local-first idea behind that is explained in [what is a local AI assistant](/what-is-local-ai).

## Does InnerZero send my health questions anywhere?

No, not by default. InnerZero runs the AI on your own machine, keeps its memory in a local database, and needs no account for the core app. Your questions about your health are processed locally and are not uploaded. Any cloud feature is opt-in and off by default, so nothing goes out unless you deliberately turn it on for a message, covered in [how InnerZero stays private](/blog/how-innerzero-stays-private).

That default is the whole point for this kind of question. You are not opting out of data collection, because there is none to opt out of for the local path.

## What can a local AI actually help with here?

Plenty of everyday, non-emergency things, as long as you treat it as a knowledgeable starting point and not a verdict. Reasonable uses include:

- Explaining a medical term or a result in plain language so you understand it before an appointment.
- Helping you write down your symptoms and questions so you make the most of a short doctor visit.
- Talking through general lifestyle topics like sleep, hydration, or building an exercise habit.
- Being a private space to put worried thoughts into words before deciding what to do.

Because it remembers across conversations, it can also keep track of context you have shared, so you do not have to re-explain your situation each time. The trade-off to keep front of mind is the next section.

## What it is not: a doctor

This part matters more than any feature. An AI assistant, local or not, is not a medical professional, cannot examine you, and can be confidently wrong. It does not diagnose, it does not prescribe, and it should never be the reason you delay getting real care. Reputable health bodies are clear that online information is no substitute for advice from a qualified clinician who knows your history ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/)).

Use it the way you might use a well-read friend: to understand things better and to prepare, not to decide. For anything serious, persistent, or worrying, and certainly for an emergency, contact a real doctor or your local emergency service. The privacy InnerZero gives you is about keeping your questions yours, not about replacing professional care.

## Can it remember my health context privately?

Yes, and locally. Because InnerZero builds memory on your machine, it can hold the context you have shared, such as an ongoing issue you are tracking, without that history being uploaded. That means a more useful conversation over time without the usual privacy cost of a remembering assistant. The wider trade-offs of local versus cloud are in [local AI vs cloud AI](/blog/local-ai-vs-cloud-ai).

You stay in control of that memory too. It is your data on your machine, so you can review or clear what it has stored whenever you want.
