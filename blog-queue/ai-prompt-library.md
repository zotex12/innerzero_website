## What is a prompt library?

A prompt library is a saved collection of reusable prompts you can pull up whenever you need them. Instead of remembering the exact wording that worked last week, or scrolling back through old chats to find it, you keep your best prompts in one place and reuse them on demand.

It sounds small, but the time adds up. Most people settle into a handful of prompts they reach for constantly. A library turns those from something you retype into something you click.

## How do I save and reuse prompts in InnerZero?

Open the Prompt Library from the Chat page. You can save a prompt with a name, and when you want it, send it straight into the chat input with a single click. Your saved text lands in the composer ready to run, or ready for you to tweak first.

That last part matters. A saved prompt is a starting point, not a locked template. Drop it in, adjust a detail for today's task, and send. You get the speed of a saved prompt with the freedom to change it.

## How do I organise my prompts?

The library keeps prompts tidy as the collection grows. You can group prompts into folders by topic, so your writing prompts, coding prompts, and research prompts stay separate. You can mark the ones you use most as favourites so they are always near the top. The aim is that the prompt you want is one or two clicks away, not buried in a long flat list.

## What prompts are worth saving?

The ones you find yourself retyping. A few that work well for most people:

- **A writing style.** "Reply in clear British English, no jargon, short paragraphs." Saves setting the tone every time.
- **A coding setup.** "You are helping with a Python project. Explain before you change code, and keep functions small."
- **A summary template.** "Summarise the text below into five bullet points and one action item."
- **A translation instruction.** "Translate the following into French, keeping the tone casual."
- **A thinking partner.** "Ask me three clarifying questions before you answer."
- **An email helper.** "Rewrite the message below to be polite, clear, and a bit shorter."
- **A meeting recap.** "Turn these rough notes into a recap with decisions and next steps."

Start with two or three you already use, and add to the library as you notice yourself repeating a prompt. For more ways to shape how the assistant behaves, see [how to customise InnerZero](/blog/customise-innerzero).

## How is this different from keeping prompts in a notes file?

You could paste your prompts into a text document, and plenty of people do. The difference is friction. A notes file means switching apps, finding the right line, copying it, and pasting it back into the chat. The Prompt Library lives inside the chat itself, so the prompt you want is one click away at the moment you need it, dropped straight into the input box. It also keeps your prompts organised in folders and favourites rather than as one long scroll, and it stays in step with the app instead of drifting out of date in a file you forget to update.

## Do I have to write good prompts from scratch?

No. The best libraries are built up over time, not written in one sitting. Start with the two or three prompts you already reach for, save them as they are, and refine them when one does not quite land. Over a week or two you end up with a small set of prompts that fit the way you actually work, each a little sharper than when you first saved it. There is no need to plan the whole collection up front, and no need to be an expert prompt writer to get value from saving the ones that already work for you.

## How many prompts should I keep?

Fewer than you might expect. A library is most useful when it holds the prompts you genuinely reach for, not every clever prompt you have ever seen. A working set of five to fifteen, grouped into a couple of folders, covers most people: a writing prompt or two, a coding setup, a summary template, and whatever is specific to your work. If a saved prompt sits unused for a month, it is fine to delete it. The aim is speed, and a short, well-chosen list is faster to use than a long one.

## Are my saved prompts private?

Yes. Like the rest of InnerZero, the Prompt Library is local. Your saved prompts live on your own machine, not in a cloud account, so the instructions you build up over time stay with you and are not uploaded anywhere. This is the same local-first approach behind the whole app, explained in [what is a local AI assistant](/what-is-local-ai).
